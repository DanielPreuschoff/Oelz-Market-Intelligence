# Signal Content Guidelines

> How to write signal cards so they are useful, consistent, and action-triggering.

## The test for every signal

Before publishing, ask: **"Would this make someone at Ölz say: I need to show this to someone, or I need to do something about this?"**

If no → the signal is noise. Don't publish it, or hold it for next edition.

---

## Headline

- Maximum 80 characters
- Plain language, no jargon
- Include the competitor name and the action
- Should work as a standalone statement without reading the summary

**Good examples:**
- "Harry-Brot lists new rye toast range at Billa Austria"
- "Penam opens second production facility in Brno"
- "Lidl CZ launches own-label croissant at €0.69"

**Bad examples:**
- "Competitor news" ← too vague
- "Harry-Brot has been observed to be expanding their product portfolio in the Austrian market" ← too long, passive
- "New product" ← meaningless

---

## Summary

- 2–5 sentences
- Structure: What happened → Scope/detail → Why it matters for Ölz
- Write for a busy reader who will spend 15 seconds on this
- Do not speculate beyond what the source confirms — if something is an inference, mark it: "This suggests..." or "Likely signals..."
- Always include at least one sentence with commercial or strategic implication

**Good example:**
> Harry-Brot has listed a new 4-pack rye toast variant in Billa and Merkur stores across Austria, priced at €1.89. The SKU targets the health-oriented breakfast segment with a "no preservatives" front-of-pack claim. This is their first new toast format in AT in 18 months and represents a direct challenge to Ölz's whole grain toast positioning. Watch for promotional price in Q2.

**Bad example:**
> Harry-Brot launched a new product. It is now in stores. The product was seen at Billa.

---

## Importance scoring

| Score | Label | Meaning |
|-------|-------|---------|
| 1 | Notable | Worth knowing, low urgency. Weak signal, trend confirmation. |
| 2 | Important | Requires attention. Could affect Ölz commercially or strategically. |
| 3 | Critical | Demands action or discussion. Major move, direct threat, or opportunity. |

**Default to 1 when uncertain.** Editors should not over-score. A 3 should feel rare.

---

## Source naming conventions

Use consistent short names for recurring sources:

| Source Name | What it is |
|-------------|------------|
| LinkedIn | LinkedIn post or job listing |
| Company website | Competitor's own press page |
| APA | APA OTS press wire (Austria) |
| Lebensmittel Zeitung | German food trade weekly |
| LEBENSMITTELpraxis | Austrian food retail magazine |
| Horizont AT | Austrian marketing trade press |
| Ihned.cz | Czech business daily |
| Retail shelf observation | Physically spotted in store |
| Trade fair | Observed at Anuga, SIAL, Internorga, etc. |

---

## AI-generated signals

- The `ai_generated` flag is set when AI was used to draft the headline or summary
- Editors must always review and edit AI drafts before marking as reviewed
- AI output is a starting point, not final copy
- Do not publish AI-drafted signals without editing — the AI will sometimes be factually wrong, overconfident, or miss the Ölz-relevant angle
